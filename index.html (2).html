Early Years — Jaipur

Roots of a dreamer

Born and raised in Jaipur, Aakash pursued engineering before earning his MBA from IMT Ghaziabad — where his fascination with entrepreneurship quietly took root alongside his coursework.

Pre-2017 — Corporate India

Dell · Airtel · Snapdeal · MobiKwik

He built a decorated corporate career across four industry giants, leading marketing at MobiKwik and gaining a rare blend of fintech, e-commerce, and consumer-tech experience. And then, at the peak of it all, he walked away.

2017 — The Leap

Zypp Electric is born

Together with his wife and co-founder Rashi Agarwal, Aakash saw what India's streets were becoming — polluted, chaotic, expensive — and bet everything on electric mobility for last-mile delivery. Starting with 50 scooters in Delhi-NCR.

2019 — The Pivot

B2C → B2B — finding the sweet spot

The team pivoted from consumer rentals to a B2B fleet model for delivery companies, rebranding to Zypp. The move unlocked partnerships with Zomato, Swiggy, Flipkart, BigBasket, Zepto, and Blinkit.

2021 — Shark Tank India Season 1

Every shark passed. The market didn't.

In one of India's most famous startup stories, the sharks declined. But Aakash proved that the market was the only judge that mattered — going on to raise $6.5 M Series A, then $25 M Series B led by Gogoro (Taiwan).

2024–2026 — Scale

India's largest EV last-mile platform

Revenue crossed ₹455 crore in FY25. Fleet expanded to 22,000+ electric vehicles across Delhi-NCR, Mumbai, Bengaluru, and Hyderabad. EBITDA profitability within sight, and an IPO on the horizon.

Rejected on Shark Tank. Vindicated by the market.

When every shark on Shark Tank India Season 1 passed on Zypp Electric's ₹2.2 crore ask, most would have wavered. Aakash doubled down. Today Zypp is widely seen as one of India's most successful EV startup stories — a testament to resilience, timing, and the courage not to need someone else's validation.

The person behind the company

Not just a founder.
A genuinely good human being.

Aakash didn't come from a family with startup capital or industry connections. He grew up in Jaipur, earned everything through hard work, built a corporate career from scratch, and when most people would have stayed comfortable, he chose the harder path. Not for glory, but because he genuinely believed in what he was building.

The early years of Zypp were not easy. Investors said no. The EV infrastructure didn't exist. Riders were skeptical. The company pivoted once, struggled through the pandemic years, got rejected on national television, and kept going anyway. That's not resilience as a talking point — that's what it actually looks like in real life.

What stands out about Aakash isn't the funding rounds or the revenue numbers. It's that through all of it, he stayed the same person. He still talks to riders directly. He still trusts young people with real work. He still shares his journey openly so others can learn from it. That's rare in anyone, let alone someone building at this scale.
